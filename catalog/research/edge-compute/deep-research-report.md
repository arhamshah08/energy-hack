# Edge Compute Management: Research Library for Site Identification, Grid Capacity, Load Management, and Distributed Compute Aggregation

## Source corpus

The source set below emphasizes U.S. primary sources published from 2022 through November 2026, with California, ERCOT, PJM, and MISO given additional coverage. Older material is included only where it remains foundational to market rules, standards, or datasets. Sources tagged **T1** cover data-center/edge-compute growth; **T2** siting difficulty; **T3** public datasets; **T4** flexibility/DERMS vocabulary and standards; **T5** tools and software; and **T6** programs. Where a source applies to more than one of the four requested categories, all applicable categories are shown.

### Contested or unverified claims

- **Market-size forecasts for “edge data centers” specifically:** vendor and consultancy forecasts frequently define “edge,” “micro data center,” “distributed data center,” and “edge computing infrastructure” differently. No single 2022–Nov. 2026 primary U.S. government dataset located in this research provides a standardized edge-data-center market size. Vendor-only market-size figures are therefore **unverified** and omitted from the key-number table.

- **“AI inference will necessarily move most compute to edge sites”:** latency, data-locality, networking and sovereignty can motivate edge deployment, but the proportion of AI inference that will migrate to edge infrastructure is architecture- and workload-dependent. No authoritative primary source located here supports a universal numerical forecast; claims assigning a specific majority share are **unverified**.

- **Exact time-to-power for an arbitrary data-center site:** utility hosting-capacity maps are screening tools, not guarantees. PG&E explicitly states its portal does not guarantee interconnection capability, cost or schedule; engineering review and permitting remain necessary.

- **Hosting-capacity values as synonymous with guaranteed load headroom:** **contested/incorrect if treated literally.** ICA values are modeled capacity estimates under specified assumptions; feeder switching, new loads, DER, planned upgrades and operating conditions can change results.

- **“Data centers are always flat loads”:** EIA uses an essentially flat server load shape in AEO2026 modeling, but field behavior can differ materially, especially for AI workloads. DOE/LBNL and PJM/ERCOT reliability work documents flexible and highly dynamic computational loads, so “always flat” is **not a general empirical fact**.

- **Universal percentage of data-center load that is flexible:** no authoritative percentage applies across all facilities. Flexibility varies by workload deadline, redundancy, IT architecture, thermal design, storage/generation, service-level agreements and grid-program rules. LBNL treats flexibility as a set of separate mechanisms rather than a single fixed percentage.

- **Specific claims of “up to 15%” electricity-cost savings from workload optimization:** this figure appears in secondary discussion citing EPRI work; because savings depend on tariff structure, regional prices and workload constraints, it should be treated as **scenario-specific rather than universal**.

- **Claims that a particular site has “available substation capacity” based solely on public maps:** **unverified until utility engineering review**. SCE and PG&E both disclaim guaranteed outcomes from their public distribution maps.

- **Claims that data-center water consumption is intrinsically high or intrinsically low:** both are overgeneralizations. LBNL finds workload-level water use varies by more than 10,000× across combinations of server efficiency, cooling, grid mix, climate and utilization.

- **Any single long-run forecast of U.S. data-center electricity use:** projections remain highly uncertain. LBNL's 2024 report gave a 2028 range of 6.7–12.0% of U.S. electricity, while the 2025 update extended the horizon and widened 2030 uncertainty to 9.5–15.3%; forecasts should therefore retain their publication date and scenario assumptions.
